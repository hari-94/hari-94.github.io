What I bring to a team

Analysts who only build charts create a queue. I try to remove one.

Every point below is something I have already shipped in a production environment — the evidence sits in the case studies further down.

End to end

I own the whole path, not just the last chart

I model raw source data into layered, version-controlled dbt marts on Snowflake, then build reporting on top of that curated layer — so the number on the dashboard has a traceable lineage instead of a one-off script behind it.

Leverage

I build self-service so the queue shrinks

At CMA-CGM I defined Looker Explores and metric definitions so PMO and infrastructure teams could pull their own ticketing and resource numbers. Requests that used to need an analyst stopped needing one.

Data quality

I check the number before someone else has to

I trace upstream discrepancies back to source tables and embed validation and tests into transformations, so dashboard metrics reconcile with the source of truth — and stakeholders stop quietly losing trust in the report.

Translation

I turn a business question into a spec

An MBA and two years in a PMO taught me to sit with stakeholders, gather requirements and convert them into KPI and metric definitions and report specifications — before any SQL gets written.

Scheduling optimization engine

github.com/hari-94/BGV-Scheduling →
1.3 hrssaved daily
338 hrsa year
$15,600cost avoided / yr
Problem
Room assignments across housekeeping staff were set by hand every morning, and quality inspectors were routed in a way that sent them back and forth between floors.
Built
A scheduling automation tool implementing custom tight-packing and greedy-packing algorithms to assign Full Clean and Daily Service rooms across staff, plus re-engineered inspector group assignment to run sequentially by floor.
Outcome
Cut 1.3 hours of manual scheduling a day and about $60 a day in operating cost, and produced a composite report the department did not previously have — room assignment moved from hand-built to algorithmic.
What it solved
  • Schedule ready in seconds. The morning plan that took over an hour by hand is built instantly.
  • Fair workloads. Every housekeeper gets a realistic, balanced day.
  • Less walking. Each person's rooms are grouped close together instead of spread across the resort.
  • Easy to use. Staff see their rooms on their phone, in English or Spanish.

Problem
Departmental budget tracking lived in manual spreadsheets, an hour of data entry a day, where the same expense could be entered twice and nobody noticed until reconciliation.
Built
A full-stack budget application on a relational database with vendor management and database-enforced duplicate-expense prevention, a live dashboard showing month-over-month variance and over-budget alerts, and automated PDF reporting for stakeholders. I owned deployment through GitHub and Streamlit Cloud.
Outcome
Removed an hour of manual Excel entry a day and gave the department visibility that curtailed expenses by at least $1,500 a month — the data now validates itself and the monthly report generates itself.
What it solved
  • One source of truth. The whole budget lives in one app instead of scattered spreadsheets.
  • No double counting. The same expense can't be entered twice.
  • Clear accountability. It shows who changed what, and when.
  • No surprises. Spending against budget is visible at a glance, with a warning before it runs over.

Labor schedule data recovery

PythonopenpyxlETLclustering
30,401records recovered
85sheets consolidated
181employees covered
Problem
Years of housekeeping scheduling history sat trapped in a single multi-year Excel workbook of roughly 85 sheets — unqueryable, and therefore unused.
Built
A Python/openpyxl ETL pipeline that cleaned and transformed the workbook into 30,401 structured shift records across 181 employees, then ran quantitative trend, clustering, travel and workload-balance comparisons across them.
Outcome
Scheduling policy could be argued from measured history rather than recollection.
What it solved
  • Years of data made usable. Scattered scheduling sheets turned into one clean dataset.
  • Patterns made visible. Who works when, and how evenly the work is shared.
  • Better planning. Staffing decisions are now based on facts, not memory.
